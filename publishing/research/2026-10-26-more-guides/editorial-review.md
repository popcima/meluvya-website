### Practical and factual assessment

**Find photos on iPhone with search and captions**

The guide explains supported search clues, useful caption wording and a repeatable retrieval sequence. It correctly distinguishes captions from visible image markup, ordinary search from optional Apple Intelligence features, and unsuccessful search from proof of deletion. The revised search-scope wording is precise. Its task is distinct from organizing albums, copying text with Live Text, changing dates and recovering deleted files.

The supplied Apple evidence supports search inputs, media-type narrowing, captions and stored dates. Examples are presented as suggested methods rather than guaranteed recognition or reported tests.

**Print photos from iPhone with AirPrint**

The procedure covers choosing the photo, selecting the printer, reviewing available options, checking the preview and printing a proof. It distinguishes network printing from Bluetooth pairing and addresses duplicate jobs through Print Center. Printer-specific paper and layout limitations are explicit.

The practical contribution is the combination of printer discovery, composition checks and queue troubleshooting. Existing print-size coverage concerns pixel dimensions and PPI; this article answers how to submit and diagnose an actual print job. The film-camera stock image is honestly labelled but contributes little instruction.

**Export iPhone photos to a USB drive**

The guide correctly separates drive visibility, destination selection, source-version choice, export completion and verification. Its explanation that an unmodified-original export excludes the appearance of edits is prominent. It also warns that Files may initially show iCloud Drive, preventing an easily misunderstood destination choice.

The supplied Apple evidence supports adapters, power needs, disk configuration and the export action. The guide avoids destructive formatting advice and does not suggest deleting the source. It adds sufficient connection and transfer detail to remain distinct from the existing sync-versus-backup and original-preservation articles.

**iCloud Shared Photo Library vs Shared Albums**

The comparison centers on joint management, contribution permissions and storage responsibility. It accurately communicates that Shared Library participants can edit or delete content and that contributed items move out of the Personal Library. Automatic camera contributions, public-link considerations and organizer storage limits receive appropriate attention.

The article explicitly sends file-quality questions to the existing Shared Albums quality guide, preserving a distinct collaboration-and-control purpose. It does not promise original-quality delivery or simplify library-exit retention rules into unsupported instructions.
